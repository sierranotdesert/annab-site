The company

Clema builds active cooling that a worker can wear through a full shift.

Our smart suspenders hold skin temperature more than 25°F below ambient across a full 8 to 12 hour shift on a single battery swap, with no ice and no water. They weigh under three pounds and are slim enough to wear under FR gear or harnesses, which is what makes them usable on a real jobsite in construction, energy, logistics, or manufacturing.

Every deployed unit also instruments a worker. That is the second product: continuous on-body data for a workforce that has never had any.

The standard

I am the only startup founder on the ISEA 600 committee.

The committee is writing OSHA's first federal heat-safety standard. I hold that seat alongside 3M and Cintas, on a rule expected to reach an estimated 36 million American workers. It is an unusual position for a company our size, and it means we are helping define the specification our category will be measured against.
